# Literature foundations for video-diffusion-supervised articulated 3D reconstruction

**The pipeline you propose — single closed-state image → video diffusion → part-decomposed 3D with internal geometry and URDF — sits at the intersection of at least four rapidly evolving subfields, and no existing work has assembled these components end-to-end.** The closest precedent is ArtHOI (Huang et al., arXiv 2603.04338, March 2026), which is the only published work using video-diffusion-generated frames for articulated 4D reconstruction — but it targets human-object interaction synthesis rather than part-decomposed 3D with URDF output. Your key novelties — jointly solving part segmentation during optimization, completing occluded internal geometry via cross-state observation, and constraining joint axes to contact-anchor sets — each have partial precedents but remain genuinely open when composed. This report critically synthesizes **70+ papers from 2022–2026** across all seven research areas, organized around the four cross-cutting challenges most critical to your paper's methodology.

## The single-image articulated reconstruction landscape has bifurcated into two paradigms

The field has split sharply between **feed-forward generative approaches** and **per-instance optimization methods**, with fundamentally different trade-offs for your pipeline.

**Feed-forward methods** now dominate in speed and scalability. PAct (Liu et al., arXiv 2602.14965, February 2026) fine-tunes TRELLIS with a part-aware flow model, producing part-decomposed meshes and joint parameters from a single image in a single forward pass. An articulation MLP aggregates multi-step diffusion features to predict joint type, axis, pivot, and motion range per part. ART (Li et al., arXiv 2512.14671, December 2025, Meta/Stanford) uses a category-agnostic transformer with learnable part-slot tokens and separate geometry/articulation decoders, requiring sparse multi-state images. LARM (Yuan & Shi et al., SIGGRAPH Asia 2025) extends the LVSM architecture for articulated novel view synthesis from sparse posed views. SINGAPO (Liu et al., ICLR 2025) generates articulated objects from single images via a DDPM conditioned on GPT-4o-inferred connectivity graphs and DINOv2 features, but uses **retrieval-based geometry** (limited by database quality). Articulate-Anything (Le et al., ICLR 2025) achieves 75% success rate across 46 categories using VLM actor-critic loops but also relies on mesh retrieval.

**Per-instance optimization methods** remain necessary when precise geometry and kinematics matter. PARIS (Liu et al., ICCV 2023) jointly optimizes dual NeRFs and motion parameters from multi-view images at two articulation states — but is notoriously unstable, with "occasional catastrophic failures" documented by multiple follow-ups. FreeArt3D (Chen et al., SIGGRAPH Asia 2025) extends SDS from 3D-to-4D by treating articulation as a generative dimension, jointly optimizing geometry, texture, and articulation against TRELLIS priors — but **requires input segmentation masks and joint type specification**. ArticulatedGS (Guo et al., CVPR 2025) and SplArt (Lin et al., ICCV 2025) represent the new 3DGS-based optimization frontier, with SplArt achieving **3.44×10⁻² axis error** (a 30× improvement over PARIS's 103.12×10⁻²) while running 100× faster.

**Critical implication for your pipeline:** Your approach most closely resembles the optimization paradigm (since you jointly solve segmentation + articulation without ground truth), but your "intermediate supervision" comes from video diffusion rather than real multi-view captures. This places you in a unique position that inherits challenges from both paradigms. The TRELLIS backbone (Xiang et al., CVPR 2025 Spotlight, Microsoft) — a structured latent 3D representation with Rectified Flow Transformers trained on 500K objects — has become the de facto generative prior for PAct, FreeArt3D, and MorphAny3D. Whether your pipeline uses TRELLIS or an alternative 3D prior will significantly impact geometry quality.

Key works that **jointly solve segmentation** (as your pipeline requires) include: PAct (emergent from part-aware generation), ART (via part-slot attention), PARIS (via motion-based separation), Ditto (via joint decoder), and the newer 3DGS methods (ArticulatedGS, SplArt, GEAR, ArtPro). Methods that still **require given segmentation** — FreeArt3D, Real2Code (Mandi et al., ICLR 2025), URDFormer (Chen et al., RSS 2024) — face a fundamental bottleneck your pipeline avoids.

## Video diffusion outputs are geometrically unreliable, but uncertainty-aware lifting is maturing

**WAN 2.2** (Team Wan, Alibaba, arXiv 2503.20314, March 2025) uses a Diffusion Transformer with Flow Matching and a novel 3D Causal VAE (Wan-VAE) achieving spatiotemporal compression with explicit temporal continuity. The Mixture-of-Experts architecture (~27B total parameters, 14B active) splits denoising into a high-noise expert (layout) and low-noise expert (details). WAN ranks #1 on VBench for motion smoothness and scene consistency. However, **no published work has used WAN specifically for downstream 3D reconstruction** as of April 2026. The closest is CHORD (Lyu et al., arXiv 2601.04194, January 2026, Stanford), which distills 4D motion from WAN 2.2 via a novel SDS target for Rectified Flow — but CHORD requires pre-existing 3D meshes and produces Lagrangian deformations, not articulated models.

The core technical challenge is **generative drift** — video diffusion models produce frames with per-frame geometric distortions that accumulate and violate multi-view consistency. This has been explicitly characterized by "World Reconstruction From Inconsistent Views" (arXiv 2603.16736, 2026), which proposes non-rigid alignment using geometric foundation models to correct drift before Gaussian Splatting optimization. Geometry Forcing (arXiv 2507.07982, 2025) takes a complementary approach, aligning video diffusion features with a pretrained geometric model (VGGT) during generation to internalize 3D consistency. Their **Revisit Error** metric (comparing first/last frames under 360° rotation) provides a principled measure of long-range consistency.

**Uncertainty quantification from diffusion-generated views** has emerged as a critical capability. Four distinct approaches exist:

- **RIGI** (arXiv 2411.18866, 2025) captures stochasticity between dual Gaussian models to estimate per-pixel uncertainty maps, then applies uncertainty-aware regularization during 3DGS reconstruction
- **UAR-Scenes** (arXiv 2503.15742, ICCV 2025) computes per-pixel entropy from MLLM-guided segmentation of generated views, yielding uncertainty-weighted reconstruction losses
- **Geo4D** (arXiv 2504.07961, 2025) repurposes video diffusion for monocular 4D reconstruction, generating multiple geometric modalities plus uncertainty maps via a dedicated decoder branch
- **Splat4D** (SIGGRAPH 2025) identifies unreliable regions via multi-view rendering comparison, then uses video diffusion to inpaint masked areas

**For your pipeline specifically**, the progression from SDS-based optimization (slow, mode-seeking, Janus artifacts) toward direct reconstruction from generated multi-view videos represents a critical design choice. SV4D 2.0 (Yao et al., ICCV 2025) eliminates SV4D's dependency on reference multi-views via random masking and introduces **3D attention conditioned on camera poses** with learnable α-blending between spatial and temporal attention — achieving 44% FV4D improvement. 4Diffusion (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2024) demonstrates that a **unified multi-view video diffusion model** avoids the consistency problems of mixing separate diffusion models. Your pipeline should strongly consider generating multi-view-consistent frames rather than single-view video, or alternatively adopt the World Reconstruction approach of post-hoc non-rigid alignment.

## The chicken-and-egg problem between segmentation and articulation has three credible solutions

This circular dependency — segmentation requires knowing articulation, but articulation estimation requires segmentation — is now the **most actively studied optimization challenge** in the field. Three strategies have emerged, each with verified strengths and documented failure modes.

**Strategy 1: EM-style alternation with external priors.** GEAR (Li et al., arXiv 2604.07728, April 2026, CAS) provides the most rigorous treatment. It treats part segmentation as a latent variable (E-step) and joint motion parameters as explicit variables (M-step), alternating refinement within a 2DGS framework. Crucially, GEAR uses **vanilla SAM priors as weak supervision** in the E-step — not category-specific fine-tuning — to prevent collapse to trivial solutions. Their convergence analysis on a 7-part storage object demonstrates that joint optimization oscillates without converging, staged optimization stagnates from irreversible early errors, while **EM alternation produces characteristic step-like error drops** during E/M transitions. The KNN clustering loss prevents "mask drifting" — a failure mode where segmentation labels gradually shift during optimization. GEAR handles up to 7+ movable parts.

**Strategy 2: Multi-stage decoupled optimization.** ArticulatedGS (Guo et al., CVPR 2025) follows four stages: (a) static Gaussian reconstruction, (b) deformation network training with ARAP regularization, (c) motion classification from deformed Gaussians, (d) joint refinement. SplArt (Lin et al., ICCV 2025) uses a 3-stage approach with a novel differentiable mobility parameter per Gaussian and cross-static/cross-mobile geometric consistency losses. SplArt achieves **99% success rate** versus PARIS's 42% on their benchmark. AiM (ICLR 2025) uses dual-Gaussian representation with motion cues from continuous video, discovering parts via sequential RANSAC without assuming known part count.

**Strategy 3: Over-segmentation with adaptive merging.** ArtPro (arXiv 2602.22666, February 2025) directly addresses the failure mode that "an initial segmentation from heuristic clustering that fails on complex multi-part objects can still yield a small rendering loss, causing optimization to converge to an incorrect kinematic structure." Their solution: start with deliberate over-segmentation, then merge mobility proposals via motion-consistency analysis and collision-aware pruning during optimization.

**Which strategy best fits your pipeline?** Your use of video-diffusion-generated frames as supervision introduces additional noise compared to real multi-view captures, making robustness paramount. The EM approach (GEAR) is most principled but requires careful initialization. The decoupled approach (ArticulatedGS/SplArt) is most empirically reliable but assumes clean multi-view images. **ArtHOI's approach** — using optical flow from video diffusion to bootstrap part segmentation via dynamic/static clustering, then densifying with SAM — is the only validated strategy for diffusion-generated inputs specifically. Their decoupled pipeline (first solve object articulation, then condition downstream tasks on it) explicitly avoids joint optimization instability under monocular ambiguity.

The identifiability question deserves attention: part segmentation from motion is **provably under-determined** when articulation is small, axes are colinear, or parts are occluded throughout the motion. Yan & Pollefeys (ECCV 2006) formalize this as linear manifold finding where degenerate cases arise from insufficient motion diversity. MotionBits (arXiv 2603.06846, 2025) introduces the concept of the smallest segmentation unit defined through kinematic spatial twist equivalence, providing a theoretical floor on what motion-based segmentation can resolve.

## Occluded internal geometry remains the least solved component of the pipeline

Completing geometry that is invisible in the closed state but becomes visible during opening is the **most under-addressed challenge** in the literature. No existing method provides a fully satisfactory solution.

**PARIS** implicitly handles this by observing the object in two states — geometry hidden when closed becomes visible when open. But PARIS does not explicitly model cross-state visibility or perform dedicated internal surface completion; it simply relies on multi-view coverage from whichever state reveals a surface. **Ditto** similarly aggregates both point clouds without explicit occlusion reasoning. Both fail when surfaces remain invisible across all observations.

**Structure from Action** (SfA, Columbia, ~2024) is the most explicit approach, using a persistent volumetric "history volume" that accumulates revealed surfaces across multiple interaction steps. An interaction policy selects informative actions specifically to discover and reveal initially occluded surfaces. However, SfA requires physical robot interaction — not applicable to your generated-video setting.

**ART** (Meta, 2025) makes the key empirical finding that **"per-part supervision is essential for learning occluded content; otherwise, the result is smeared geometry and inferior texture quality."** By supervising each part independently rather than the composite image, the model is forced to predict complete per-part geometry even when parts are partially occluded. This is directly relevant to your pipeline — if your optimization decomposes the object into parts, per-part reconstruction losses should be enforced.

**For shape completion of never-observed regions**, the most promising approaches are:

- **ConvONet** (Peng et al., ECCV 2020) propagates information from observed to unobserved regions via U-Net receptive fields on triplane/volume features — the backbone used by Ditto
- **ShapeFormer** (Yan et al., CVPR 2022) generates a **distribution** of plausible completions using a transformer autoregressive model with VQDIF representation, capturing multi-modal ambiguity
- **SC-Diff** (2024) applies latent diffusion on TSDF-space for shape completion, outperforming class-specific methods by **12%** and class-agnostic by **47%**
- **Amodal3R** (Wu et al., ICCV 2025) builds on TRELLIS for complete 3D reconstruction from partially occluded images, using mask-weighted cross-attention biased toward visible regions plus an occlusion-aware attention layer

**Visibility-aware fusion** provides the theoretical framework for weighting observations across states. Neural Visibility Fields (NVF, Xue et al., CVPR 2024) use Bayesian networks to model NeRF volume rendering, assigning higher uncertainty to unobserved regions. SCFusion (Wu et al., 3DV 2021) uses explicit known/unknown voxel states with integration policies that merge completions at lower confidence than direct observations. DreamArt (SIGGRAPH Asia 2025) uses "part amodal completion" — explicitly reconstructing parts including hidden sides from a single image using diffusion priors.

**A practical recommendation for your pipeline:** Implement a two-tier completion strategy. First, use cross-state visibility tracking to identify which voxels are observed in which generated frames, with uncertainty weights derived from frame reliability (via RIGI-style dual-model uncertainty or UAR-Scenes-style entropy). Second, for regions that remain unobserved across all frames, apply a learned shape completion module (SC-Diff or ConvONet-based) conditioned on the partially observed part geometry. The per-part supervision principle from ART should guide your loss design.

## Joint axis estimation benefits from screw theory parameterization and contact-boundary constraints

The parameterization of joint axes is a subtle but consequential design choice. Three representations compete in the literature, and the evidence favors screw/Plücker coordinates.

**Direct regression** (Ditto, ANCSH) predicts axis direction and pivot as continuous 3D vectors from neural network outputs. This is straightforward but **lacks geometric structure** — the network must learn that axis and pivot are coupled. ANCSH (Li et al., CVPR 2020) mitigates this by predicting in a Normalized Articulated Object Coordinate Space, but remains category-specific. Ditto's axis direction is regressed from fused point features without explicit constraints, leading to occasional wrong joint type predictions.

**Screw theory / Plücker coordinates** provide a **unified, singularity-free representation**. A screw axis is parameterized as (ω, v) ∈ ℝ⁶ where ω is direction and v = q×ω is the moment. Revolute joints have ‖ω‖=1, h=0 (zero pitch); prismatic joints have ω=0. The exponential map e^{[S]θ} ∈ SE(3) provides differentiable transformation. ScrewNet (Jain et al., ICRA 2021) predicts screw sequences from depth image streams, deducing joint type from learned parameters. **ScrewSplat** (Kim et al., CoRL 2025) achieves the most elegant formulation: n_s screw primitives each with Plücker coordinates and a **confidence score γ ∈ [0,1]**, optimized end-to-end with 3DGS via a parsimony loss β∑√γ_j that automatically discovers the number of joints. NAP (Lei et al., NeurIPS 2023) also uses Plücker coordinates in its diffusion-based generative model. Ehrig et al. (J. Biomechanics, 2006) demonstrated that Plücker-based screw axis estimation is **superior to point-transformation methods under measurement noise** — directly relevant to your noisy diffusion-generated inputs.

**Classification over discrete geometric primitives** offers a third path. Real2Code (Mandi et al., ICLR 2025) converts joint estimation into selecting among OBB axes and edges via a fine-tuned LLM, avoiding continuous regression entirely. This scales elegantly to 10+ parts but **accuracy is fundamentally limited by OBB discretization granularity**.

**The contact-boundary constraint** — that a revolute axis must pass through the interface between base and movable part — is physically grounded but **surprisingly under-exploited in the literature**. ANCSH enforces kinematic constraints during pose optimization via energy minimization. MotionAnymesh (2025) uses type-aware geometric initialization where the initial revolute axis is estimated from the contact interface between segmented parts, followed by physics-constrained trajectory refinement. DTA (CVPR 2024) uses physically-based collision minimization alongside 3D geometry consistency. However, **no existing method explicitly parameterizes the axis as passing through a discrete set of anchor voxels on the contact boundary** — this appears to be a genuine novelty in your proposed approach.

The anchor-set concept has precedent in A2J (Xiong et al., ICCV 2019), which uses densely placed anchor points for human joint regression via weighted aggregation. Adapting this to rotation axis estimation — where the axis must pass through a set of contact-boundary voxels — would provide both a physically grounded constraint and a differentiable parameterization (the axis is the least-squares line through weighted anchor positions). FlowBot3D (Eisner et al., RSS 2022) provides complementary evidence: its per-point 3D Articulation Flow encodes joint information implicitly, and axis recovery amounts to fitting the line about which flow vectors are tangential. This fitting is most reliable when performed on points near the contact boundary.

**Documented failure modes across all methods include:** axis drift during optimization (PARIS), symmetric ambiguity where round knobs provide no geometric signal for axis position, thin/small parts that corrupt segmentation and downstream joint estimation, and the fundamental under-determination of joint parameters from static observations without observed motion.

## Flow-based motion supervision from diffusion outputs is an emerging but fragile bridge

Using optical flow between video-diffusion-generated frames as supervision for articulated reconstruction is an extremely recent idea. **ArtHOI is the only published validation** of this concept. Its pipeline — point tracking → dynamic/static clustering by flow magnitude → SAM densification → back-projection to 3D Gaussians — achieves ~5.62° mean rotation error for articulated dynamics. But ArtHOI operates on relatively simple single-joint articulations (fridges, cabinets) and acknowledges that optical flow tracking failures on complex motions remain a key limitation.

The broader flow-for-articulation literature provides strong foundations. LASR (Yang et al., CVPR 2021) and BANMo (Yang et al., CVPR 2022) demonstrate that forward-rendered optical flow compared against off-the-shelf flow estimates (VCN-robust) generates effective gradient signals for articulated shape optimization. ViSER (Yang et al., NeurIPS 2021) establishes long-range correspondences via learned surface embeddings rather than multi-frame flow chaining — important because flow chaining accumulates drift. The "Flow Supervision for Deformable NeRF" paper provides an analytical solution for enforcing flow constraints on backward deformation fields, solving a non-trivial mathematical problem that the community had previously sidestepped.

**Velocity field approaches** offer a complementary perspective. NVFi (Neural Velocity Fields) learns time-dependent 3D velocity for every point, enabling scene decomposition — points with consistent rigid velocity are grouped into the same part. ReMatching (arXiv 2411.00705) frames dynamic reconstruction as projection onto velocity-field prior classes via flow matching, bridging generative flow matching with physics-based reconstruction. EulerFlow (Vedder et al., 2024) models scene flow as an ODE, achieving supervised-level performance without supervision on Argoverse 2.

**For your pipeline**, the critical concern is that **flow estimated from diffusion-generated videos may be significantly less reliable than from real videos**. Diffusion models can produce temporally inconsistent textures, flickering, and hallucinated motion that corrupt optical flow estimation. Mitigation strategies include: (1) using robust flow estimators that handle noise (RAFT with confidence outputs), (2) uncertainty-weighted flow supervision where unreliable frames contribute less, (3) flow consensus across multiple diffusion samples (generating multiple videos from the same prompt and aggregating flow fields), and (4) the Bayesian fusion framework from Liao et al. (WACV 2024) for selecting the N lowest-uncertainty observations. sim2art (arXiv 2512.07698, 2025) — the first data-driven approach jointly inferring part segmentation and joint parameters from single monocular video — uses DETR-inspired assignment but was trained exclusively on synthetic data, suggesting domain gap issues with diffusion-generated frames.

## Benchmarks and metrics are converging but not yet standardized

**PartNet-Mobility** (Xiang et al., CVPR 2020/SAPIEN) is the dominant benchmark with ~2,346 articulated models across 46 categories and ~14,068 annotated joints, providing URDF-compatible kinematic annotations. Nearly every paper reviewed uses it. However, it contains synthetic CAD models with limited texture realism. **GAPartNet** (Geng et al., CVPR 2023) complements with **8,489 part instances** across 9 actionable part classes, emphasizing cross-category generalization (17 seen / 10 unseen split). **AKB-48** (Liu et al., CVPR 2022) provides **2,037 real-world scanned objects** across 48 categories with physical properties — superior for sim-to-real evaluation.

The de facto metric suite includes: **axis angular error** (degrees, threshold ~14.3° or 0.25 rad), **axis position error** (meters, threshold ~0.15m), **joint type accuracy** (binary classification), **part segmentation IoU/mIoU**, **Chamfer Distance** (whole-object and per-part), and **PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS** for visual quality. LARM proposes the most systematic composite evaluation: success requires simultaneously satisfying thresholds on axis angle, axis origin, motion range, and motion direction. SplArt introduces the SPLART-PMS benchmark with 22 objects featuring continuous articulation states. No fully standardized protocol exists, but reporting all six core metrics above would satisfy reviewers at top venues.

The comprehensive survey by Liu, Savva, & Mahdavi-Amiri (Computer Graphics Forum, 2025, arXiv 2403.14937) provides the most thorough field overview, noting that "the geometric decomposition of parts lays the foundational groundwork for analyzing part mobility, while articulation modeling guides shape understanding."

## Conclusion: four genuinely open problems and where your contributions fit

**Open Problem 1: Video-diffusion frames as articulation supervision.** ArtHOI (2026) is the sole validation point. No work has demonstrated this for multi-part objects, internal geometry completion, or URDF output. The uncertainty quantification methods (RIGI, UAR-Scenes, Geo4D) exist for static reconstruction but have not been applied to articulated settings. This is genuinely open territory.

**Open Problem 2: Contact-anchor-constrained axis estimation.** The screw/Plücker representation is well-validated (ScrewNet, ScrewSplat, NAP), and the contact-boundary principle is physically grounded, but **no existing method parameterizes the rotation axis as passing through discrete anchor voxels on the part interface**. This is a clear novelty. Combining ScrewSplat's differentiable screw parameterization with an anchor-set constraint would be technically novel and physically motivated.

**Open Problem 3: Internal geometry completion from generated intermediate states.** Existing methods either assume two-state observation suffices (PARIS, Ditto), use physical interaction (SfA), or hallucinate from generative priors (DreamArt, Amodal3R). Your pipeline's unique advantage — access to a continuous sequence of generated intermediate states — could enable **progressive visibility-tracked geometry accumulation** that no existing method exploits. The known/unknown voxel framework from SCFusion combined with per-part supervision from ART provides the theoretical scaffolding.

**Open Problem 4: Robust joint optimization under diffusion noise.** GEAR's EM alternation with SAM priors represents the state-of-the-art for breaking the segmentation-articulation circular dependency, but has only been tested with real multi-view captures. Whether EM convergence guarantees hold under the additional noise from diffusion-generated frames is unstudied. The over-segmentation-then-merge strategy (ArtPro) may be more robust to noisy inputs, as it avoids committing to a segmentation early. Your paper should empirically compare these strategies under diffusion noise — this ablation alone would be a significant contribution.

A point of critical honesty: several claims in recent papers deserve scrutiny. PAct's "part-decomposed generation from single image" has not been validated on real-world images outside PartNet-Mobility categories. ScrewSplat's "end-to-end" claim still requires knowing the observation states. SINGAPO's GPT-4o connectivity inference is brittle for unusual objects. The 3DGS revolution (ArticulatedGS, SplArt, GEAR, ArtPro, DeGSS, ScrewSplat — all from 2025–2026) has produced impressive numbers but all operate on clean multi-view captures with known poses. **Your pipeline's reliance on video-diffusion-generated frames introduces noise characteristics that none of these methods have been stress-tested against.** This is both your primary risk and your primary contribution opportunity.
